Why compare with PdfBleu

🔍

Word-level precision

The comparison works word by word, not page by page: a single corrected figure in a long contract is one small change entry, not a whole flagged page.

🖼️

Visual overlay too

Some changes are not words — a moved logo, a swapped chart, a new stamp. The Content Overlay mode stacks any two pages and paints every differing pixel in bright pink.

🔒

Private by design

Drafts under comparison are often the documents you least want on someone else's server — negotiations, contracts, manuscripts. Here nothing is uploaded at all: the whole comparison runs on your device.

Frequently asked questions

How does the text comparison work?

Both documents are opened in your browser and their stored text is extracted page by page. Pages are lined up in order — page 1 against page 1, and so on — and each pair of pages is compared word by word. Words that disappeared are listed as Old changes and highlighted in red on the first document; words that appeared are New changes, highlighted in green on the second. If one document has more pages, the extra pages are reported as whole-page additions or removals.

What do the colours mean?

In the text view, red marks text that is in the first document but not in the second (removed or replaced), and green marks text that is new in the second document. In the Content Overlay, differing pixels are painted bright pink over a faded copy of the second page — anything that did not change stays pale and quiet.

My documents are scans. Why does the text comparison find nothing?

A scanned page is a photograph: it contains no stored text for any tool to read, however clear it looks. That is a property of the file, not a failure of the comparison. You have two routes: the Content Overlay tab compares the pages as pictures and still shows you what moved, and OCR PDF can recognize the text in the scans first — after that, the text comparison works on them like any other PDF.

The two documents have different page counts. What happens?

Pages are compared in order up to the shorter document. Every extra page on either side is reported as one whole-page change — an added page in the second document, or a removed page that only exists in the first. One inserted page near the start will shift the pairing for everything after it, which the report will honestly show as changes on the following pages; that is the known behaviour of in-order comparison, and the overlay tab lets you verify any page pair by hand.

What is in the downloaded report?

A plain PDF listing the two file names, the date, the summary counts, and then every change grouped by page — the removed text in red, the added text in green — so you can file it, print it, or send it to whoever made the edits. Characters that the report's font cannot represent are replaced with “?” and the replacement count is stated on the report itself.

Are my documents uploaded anywhere?

No. Both files are opened, read and compared entirely in your browser, on your device. Nothing is sent to a server — there is not even an upload step in the code — which makes the tool safe to use on contracts, drafts and other documents that should never leave your hands.
