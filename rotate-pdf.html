How to rotate a PDF

1

Add your PDFs

Click the select button or drop your files into the area provided. Every page of every file appears below as a thumbnail you can inspect.

2

Turn the pages you need

Use the arrows under a thumbnail to rotate just that page, or pick All, Portrait or Landscape in the panel and turn many pages across every file at once.

3

Check the badges

Pages you have turned carry a small badge like +90°, so you always know exactly what will change. Changed your mind? “Reset all” puts every page back.

4

Save and download

Press “Rotate PDF” and save each fixed file on its own, or download all of them together in a single ZIP file.

Frequently asked questions

Does rotating a PDF reduce its quality?

No. Rotating here only updates each page's rotation setting stored inside the file — the content itself (text, images, fonts) is never re-encoded, resampled or compressed. The rotated file looks exactly like the original, just turned the right way.

Can I rotate just one page?

Yes. Every thumbnail has its own left and right arrows, so you can turn a single page — handy for a scan where only one sheet went through the feeder sideways — and leave everything else untouched.

What do the Portrait and Landscape buttons do?

They choose which pages the big Left and Right buttons will turn: only pages that currently stand taller than wide (portrait), only pages wider than tall (landscape), or every page. “Currently” includes turns you already applied by hand, so you can fix mixed documents step by step.

Is the rotation permanent?

Yes — the new orientation is written into the downloaded file, so its pages open the right way up in any reader, on any device. Your original file is never modified: you download a rotated copy and keep whichever version you prefer.

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Your PDFs are opened, previewed and rotated entirely on your own device — nothing is sent anywhere. That is why the tool is safe for sensitive documents: contracts, ID scans and personal files never pass through a third-party server.
