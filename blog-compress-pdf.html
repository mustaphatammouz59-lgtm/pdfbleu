How to compress a PDF without ruining it

"Your file is too large." Whether it is an email limit, a government portal capped at 2 MB, or a job application form, an oversized PDF always seems to appear at the worst moment. Compression fixes it — but done blindly, it can also turn a crisp document into a blurry mess. This guide explains why PDFs get heavy and how to shrink yours while keeping it perfectly readable.

Why is your PDF so big?

  • Scans. A scanned page is a photograph. A few pages photographed at high resolution easily reach 10–30 MB.
  • Embedded photos. Reports and presentations often carry full-size camera images squeezed into small frames.
  • Repeated elements. Some programs embed the same logo, background or font many times over.

Plain text, on the other hand, weighs almost nothing: a 100-page text-only PDF can be smaller than a single photo. That is why compression helps scans and image-heavy files dramatically — and barely touches text documents.

How compression works here

The Compress PDF tool rebuilds each page at an optimized resolution inside your browser and produces a new, lighter file. Nothing is uploaded: the whole operation happens on your device, which matters when the document is a passport scan or a signed contract.

Step by step

1. Open your file in the tool

Drag your PDF into the Compress PDF page, or tap to select it. The tool shows the original size, so you can judge the improvement afterwards.

2. Compress

Press the compress button and wait a few seconds — large scanned documents take a little longer, because every page is re-rendered on your device.

3. Check before you send

Download the result and open it. Zoom into one page with small text: if it reads comfortably, you are done. Compare the new size with the limit you were given — most portals accept anything under their cap, so there is no need to crush the file further than necessary.

Keeping quality: three rules

  • Compress once. Re-compressing an already compressed file degrades it a little more each time. Always start from the original.
  • Match the target. For an email attachment, moderate compression is plenty. Save aggressive settings for strict upload caps.
  • Keep the original archived. A compressed file is a delivery copy. The full-quality original stays your master version.

One honest warning

Compressed files from this tool are optimized for sharing and storage: their pages are image-based, which means the text is no longer selectable or searchable. If your document must stay searchable — for example an official report people will quote from — keep and share the original instead, or split it with Split PDF to send it in lighter parts. If weight comes from pages you do not even need, Remove Pages shrinks the file with zero quality loss.

Frequently asked questions

How much smaller will my file get?

Scanned and photo-heavy PDFs often shrink by 70–90%. Text-only PDFs are already compact and may barely change — that is normal, not a failure.

Is there a size limit?

PdfBleu sets none. Very large files depend on your device's memory, because processing is local.

Why did my text stop being selectable?

Because compressed pages are image-based. Keep the original whenever you need selectable text.
