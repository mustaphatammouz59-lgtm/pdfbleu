    A good summary is a shortcut, not a replacement: it tells you what a report, essay or study guide is really about, and whether the rest is worth your time. This tool builds that shortcut by reading the text of your PDF, weighing every sentence against the document as a whole, and keeping the ones that carry the subject — in the document's own words, in its own order.

    Because the analysis runs on your device, the tool suits documents that should never sit on a stranger's server: contracts, internal reports, coursework, personal letters. There is no account to create and no word limit to hit; a long document may simply take a few seconds longer on a phone.

    Frequently asked questions

    How does this summarizer work?

    The text of your PDF is read in your browser, page by page, and split into sentences. Each sentence is scored by how often its important words come back across the whole document, where it sits, whether it opens a paragraph, and how much it overlaps with the document's title. Near-duplicates are thinned out so the summary does not say the same thing twice, and the winners are put back in their original order. No text is generated: the summary is assembled from the document itself.

    Is my document uploaded?

    No — everything runs on your device. The file is opened by your browser, its text is analyzed in this page, and the summary is produced locally. Nothing is sent to a server at any point, which is exactly why this tool can be used on private documents with confidence.

    Why does the summary quote my document word-for-word?

    Because it is an extract, not a rewrite. A cloud chatbot writes new sentences and can quietly invent details that were never in your file. This summarizer only selects real sentences, exactly as written, so every claim in the summary can be traced back to the document — the key points even carry the page each sentence came from.

    My PDF is a scan — what do I do?

    A scanned PDF is a set of pictures: it holds no text for any summarizer to read, and this page will tell you so instead of inventing a summary. Run the file through OCR PDF first — it recognizes the text and adds a searchable layer — then bring the result back here and summarize it as usual.

    Which languages work?

    English, French and Arabic get full handling: their common function words are recognized and set aside so the scoring concentrates on the words that carry meaning, and Arabic text is normalized before matching. Other languages still summarize — the same sentence statistics apply — with basic handling based on the English function-word list.

    Are there limits?

    No word limit like other tools impose, and no page cap either. The honest trade-off is your device: a very long document may take a few seconds to read and score on a phone, and a document with fewer than about forty words of readable text is simply too short — or too image-based — to summarize.

    Can I chat with my PDF or ask questions?

    No — that needs a cloud AI. Answering questions about a document means sending its text to an external model, and this site's promise is that your file never leaves your device. That feature is honestly excluded so your document stays private; what you get instead is a faithful, checkable summary built from the document's own sentences.
